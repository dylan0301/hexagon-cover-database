\section{A Guide to the Proof}
\label{sec:introduction}

This section gives the reader the entire proof architecture before any long
calculation.  It introduces the geometric objects and case labels, states the
exhaustive routing table, and previews the three certificate types, retained
as four named strategies, that close its rows.
Section~\ref{sec:structural-reductions} proves that the labels and reductions
are exhaustive, and Section~\ref{sec:signed-ce12-reductions} gives the common
signed center model.  The reader-facing proofs then appear in
Sections~\ref{sec:length}--\ref{sec:strategy4-reader}, before the final audit in
Section~\ref{sec:assembly}; the irreducible exact calculations are retained in
the appendices.

\subsection{The theorem}

Let $H$ be the regular hexagon of side length one.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]
\label{thm:main}
The hexagon $H$ cannot be covered by seven open equilateral triangles of side
length one.
\end{theorem}

The compactness and scaling argument in
Proposition~\ref{prop:open-closed-scaled} gives the equivalent closed form.

\begin{corollary}[Expanded closed formulation]
\label{cor:expanded-closed}
For every $L>1$, the regular hexagon $H_L$ of side length $L$ cannot be
covered by seven closed unit equilateral triangles.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{A geometric dictionary}
\label{subsec:reader-dictionary}

Indices are taken in $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$.  Put
\[
 O=(0,0),\qquad
 V_i=\left(\cos\frac{i\pi}{3},\sin\frac{i\pi}{3}\right),\qquad
 H=\conv\{V_0,\ldots,V_5\}.
\]
Write $e_{i,i+1}=[V_i,V_{i+1}]$ for a boundary edge,
$r_i=[O,V_i]$ for a radial arm, and $M_i=V_i/2$.  The radial target and the
full skeleton are
\[
 D=\bigcup_{i=0}^5r_i,\qquad S=\partial H\cup D,
\]
with lengths $\mathcal H^1(\partial H)=\mathcal H^1(D)=6$ and
$\mathcal H^1(S)=12$.

Seven distinguished points force seven distinct roles: $U_C$ contains $O$
and $U_i$ contains $V_i$.  Their closures are denoted by $T_C$ and $T_i$.
The distinction is essential because closing an open trace may add an
endpoint or a tangency.

For a vertex role, let $o$ be the number of its triangle vertices outside
$H$ and $n$ the number of adjacent arms met in positive length.  The
exhaustive names are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{type}&(o,n)\\ \hline
\Vdzero&(1,0),(2,0),(3,0)\\
\Vdone&(1,1)\\
\Vdtwo&(1,2)\\
\Tlike&(2,1).
\end{array}
\]
These classifications are mathematical rather than pictorial; the proofs of
exhaustiveness appear in
Propositions~\ref{prop:ce-classification} and
\ref{prop:vertex-classification}.

For an original open vertex role, let $A_i,B_i,C_i$ be its actual maximal
incoming boundary, outgoing boundary, and inward radial reaches.  Thus row
$i$ is \emph{supercritical} when $A_i+B_i>1$, and
\[
 N_+=\left\lvert\left\{i:A_i+B_i>1\right\}\right\rvert.
\]
Lowercase $(a_i,b_i,c_i)$ always denotes smaller selected demands, never the
actual maxima used to define $N_+$.

For the CE1/CE2 case tree, call a vertex role \emph{short} if it is
supercritical or has positive-length trace on an adjacent radial arm.  Let
$m$ be the number of roles of type $\Vdone$, $\Vdtwo$, or $\Tlike$, and let
$q$ be the number of short roles.  Lemma~\ref{lem:short-role-count} proves
\[
 q=N_++m.
\]
Thus the first CE1/CE2 routing row below removes every branch with three short
roles before any finer split is made.  In particular, the midpoint rescuer of
Lemma~\ref{lem:positive-support-rescuer} puts every $N_+\ge2$ branch in that
row.

On $e_{i,i+1}$, parametrized from $V_i$ to $V_{i+1}$, the two incident open
traces are $[0,B_i)$ and $(1-A_{i+1},1]$.  Their nonempty complement
\[
 [B_i,1-A_{i+1}]
\]
is a boundary gap.  Equality gives a singleton gap, which remains uncovered
because both incident traces are open at that point.

disjoint as written.  The CE1/CE2 rows are applied from top to bottom: after
the first row one has $q\le2$, so each later refinement is mutually
exclusive.  The one row marked $1+2$ is explicitly assigned jointly to
Strategies~1 and~2.  Exhaustiveness is proved in
Section~\ref{sec:structural-reductions}, and the final audit appears in
Section~\ref{sec:assembly}.

Within the two all-$\Vdzero$ CE1/CE2 rows, let $\mathrm{gr}$ be the
active-gap rank, the number of center traces containing V-gaps.  The proof
uses one $2\times3$ kernel:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
 &\mathrm{gr}=0&\mathrm{gr}=1&\mathrm{gr}=2\\ \hline
N_+=0&\text{strict identity cycle}&\text{one-side endpoint chain}&
       \text{paired endpoint chain}\\
N_+=1&\text{nine-point support}&\text{five-row relaxed chain}&
       \text{paired endpoint chain}.
\end{array}
\]
The last column is CE2-only.  Thus the same paired endpoint theorem closes
both two-gap cells.

The four named strategies fall into three certificate classes.
Strategies~1 and~3 are additive deficits, Strategy~2 is an isotone transfer
certificate, and Strategy~4 is a convex support certificate.  The names and
routing assignments are retained because they make the case tree easiest to
read.

\subsection{Three certificate types and four named strategies}

\paragraph{Strategy 1: one-dimensional trace budgets.}
Choose either $\partial H$ or the conditional skeleton $S$.  Bound the trace
of each role on that target according to its center class, vertex type, and
supercritical status.  In every routed branch the seven upper bounds add to
strictly less than the target length.  Section~\ref{sec:length} gives the
trace bounds and terminal contradictions.

\paragraph{Strategy 2: relaxations of \(g\)-composition chains.}
For incoming boundary defect $x$ and radial demand $c$, the historical raw map
is
\[
 g_c(x)=\max\{y:(1-x,y,c)\in\mathcal A\}.
\]
The nonsupercritical map is
\[
 \widehat g_c(x)=\min\{g_c(x),x\},
\]
and the complementary reach-coordinate transfer is
$f^\vee(a)=1-f(1-a)$.  Thus ordinary proof chains use
$\widehat g_c^\vee$, center intervals add a subscript $J$, and affine or
threshold relaxations add superscripts.  A strict-supercritical row is
summarized by the single outgoing envelope $g_c^{\rm sc}$ and its complement
$1-g_c^{\rm sc}$.  At zero radial demand,
\[
 g_0(x)>x\quad(0<x<1),
 \qquad
 \widehat g_0(x)=x;
\]
the identity belongs to the hatted map, not to the raw historical map.
Section~\ref{sec:universal-calculus} develops the notation, and
Section~\ref{sec:strategy2-reader} gives one table of all chain relaxations.
Appendix~\ref{sec:exact-local-demand-calculus} retains the aliases
$B_c,F_c,G_c$ only where they shorten the exact contact-cell formulas.

\paragraph{Strategy 3: area loss.}
Normalize a unit triangle to area one, so $H$ has area six.  Each selected
boundary handoff forces a square amount of a vertex triangle outside the
local hexagonal wedge.  Two ascents, or one ascent together with a
$\Tlike$ row, force total loss greater than one; after adding the center
triangle, the available inside area is strictly less than six.
Section~\ref{sec:area} proves the local inequalities and both cyclic sums.

\paragraph{Strategy 4: a direct nine-point obstruction.}
In the zero-gap, all-$\Vdzero$, exact-one state, the five nonsupercritical
handoffs form one strict descent chain.  It produces common lower demands
that force six radial points and three asymmetric points into $U_C$.
Their convex hull cannot fit in an open unit equilateral triangle: the same
enclosure gauge $\Lambda$ used for local demand geometry gives side at least
one, whereas compact containment in $U_C$ would give side strictly below one.
The reader-level construction and enclosure argument are in
Section~\ref{sec:strategy4-reader}; Appendix~\ref{sec:ab-core} contains the
exact frontier and overlap certificates.

The proof uses no global noncoverage claim for $S$, no interval subdivision,
and no branch-and-bound.  The last mixed-overlap step is an exact
computer-verified Bernstein certificate; its precise mathematical input,
output, and rational reduction are proved in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:exact-mixed-overlap}.
