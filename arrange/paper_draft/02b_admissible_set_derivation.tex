\section{Derivation of the Local Admissible Set}
\label{sec:admissible-set-derivation}

This section proves the explicit description stated in
Proposition~\ref{prop:body-admissible-description}.  It also derives the three
polynomial cells used by the exact-demand appendix and records the component
selectors introduced when a positive support equation is squared.

Let
\[
 u=\left(\frac12,\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right),\qquad
 v=\left(\frac12,-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right),
\]
and put
\[
 O=0,\qquad A=au,\qquad B=bv,\qquad C=c(u+v),
 \qquad K=\conv\{O,A,B,C\}.
\]
Set
\[
 s=a+b,\qquad d=a^2+ab+b^2.
\]

\subsection{The triangular hull}

Assume first that $s>0$ and $cs\le ab$.  If $a,b>0$, then
\[
 \frac ca+\frac cb\le1,
\]
so
\[
 C=\frac caA+\frac cbB+\left(1-\frac ca-\frac cb\right)O
 \in\conv\{O,A,B\}.
\]
The zero-coordinate cases follow by continuity.  The distance $AB$ equals
$\sqrt d$ and is the diameter of $\triangle OAB$.  Put
\[
 T=-bu-av.
\]
The triangle $\conv\{A,B,T\}$ is equilateral of side $\sqrt d$, and
\[
 O=\frac{b^2}{d}A+\frac{a^2}{d}B+\frac{ab}{d}T.
\]
Thus it contains $O$ and therefore also $C$.  Every enclosing set has diameter
at least $AB$, so
\[
 L_{\min}(a,b,c)=\sqrt d.
\]
When $a=b=0$, the hull is the segment $[0,c(u+v)]$ and its least enclosing
equilateral side is $c$.

\subsection{The quadrilateral hull and the four calipers}

Assume now that $s>0$ and $cs>ab$.  The four hull vertices occur in the cyclic
order
\[
 O,\ B,\ C,\ A.
\]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:universal-enclosure-gauge}, a minimizing orientation
has an outward normal perpendicular to one of the four hull edges.

For the edge $OA$, the three supports give
\[
 L_{OA}=b+\max\{a,c\};
\]
reflection gives
\[
 L_{BO}=a+\max\{b,c\}.
\]
For the edge $AC$, put
\[
 R_a=\sqrt{a^2-ac+c^2}.
\]
An outward unit normal is
\[
 n_0=\frac1{2R_a}(\sqrt3a,\,2c-a).
\]
If $x_+=\max\{x,0\}$, the support values for
$n_0,\mathsf Rn_0,\mathsf R^2n_0$ are
\[
 \frac{\sqrt3ac}{2R_a},\qquad
 \frac{\sqrt3a(a-c)_+}{2R_a},\qquad
 \frac{\sqrt3c\max\{b,(c-a)_+\}}{2R_a}.
\]
Multiplying their sum by $2/\sqrt3$ gives
\[
 L_{AC}=
 \begin{cases}
  (a^2+bc)/R_a,&a\ge c,\\
  c(a+b)/R_a,&a<c\le a+b,\\
  c^2/R_a,&c>a+b.
 \end{cases}
\]
Reflection exchanges $a$ and $b$ and gives $L_{CB}$.  These four hull edges are
exhaustive, so
\[
 L_{\min}(a,b,c)=\min\{L_{OA},L_{BO},L_{AC},L_{CB}\}.
\]
This proves Proposition~\ref{prop:body-admissible-description}, including all
equality and degenerate cases by continuity.

\subsection{Equivalent polynomial cells}

For the exact transfer calculations put
\[
 m=\min\{a,b\},\qquad M=\max\{a,b\},
 \qquad q=s^4-s^2+ab,
\]
and define
\begin{align*}
 F_L(a,b,c)&=c^4-c^2+mc-m^2,\\
 F_T(a,b,c)&=(s^2-1)c^2+Mc-M^2,\\
 F_S(a,b,c)&=(m^2-1)c^2+(2mM^2+M)c+M^4-M^2.
\end{align*}
We prove
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal A=\mathcal A_L\cup\mathcal A_T\cup\mathcal A_S,
 \label{eq:derived-admissible-cells}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal A_L&=\{d\le1,\ s\le1,\ q\le0,\ F_L\le0\},\\
 \mathcal A_T&=\{d\le1,\ s\le1,\ q\ge0,\ F_T\le0,\ c\le2M\},\\
 \mathcal A_S&=\{d\le1,\ s>1,\ F_S\le0,\ c\le1/2\},
\end{align*}
with all variables in $[0,1]$.

Assume by symmetry that $a=m\le b=M$.  Reading the active support points in
the four-caliper formulas gives the following contact patterns:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{range}&\text{active supports}&\text{unit-frontier equation}\\ \hline
s<1,\ q<0&\{O\},\{C\},\{A,C\}&F_L=0\\
s<1,\ q>0&\{O\},\{B,C\},\{A\}&F_T=0\\
s=1&\text{preceding pattern with an }O,B\text{ tie}&c=M\\
s>1&\{B\},\{B,C\},\{A\}&F_S=0.
\end{array}
\]
The inactive-support inequalities reduce respectively to
$q\le0$, $q\ge0$, and $s>1$.  The side-length equations have positive sides;
squaring them gives exactly $F_L=0,F_T=0,F_S=0$.  It remains to identify the
geometric connected component after squaring.

For Cell $T$, $0<s<1$, the roots of $F_T=0$ are
\[
 c_- =\frac{2M}{1+\sqrt{4s^2-3}},
 \qquad
 c_+ =\frac{2M}{1-\sqrt{4s^2-3}}.
\]
The inequality $F_T\le0$ contains the interval $0\le c\le c_-$ and the formal
high component $c\ge c_+$.  The geometric component is the one connected to
$c=0$.  One has
\[
 c_-\le2M\le c_+,
\]
with equality of all three quantities only when the two roots coalesce.  Hence
\[
 c\le2M
\]
selects exactly the component containing $c=0$.  Equivalently one may use
$M+2(s^2-1)c\ge0$.

For Cell $S$,
\[
 F_S(a,b,0)=M^2(M^2-1)<0,
 \qquad
 F_S(a,b,M)=M^2(s^2-1)>0.
\]
Furthermore $4F_S(a,b,1/2)$ is increasing in $m$, and at $m=1-M$ it equals
\[
 M^2(2M-1)^2.
\]
Because $s>1$ gives $m>1-M$, one has $F_S(a,b,1/2)>0$.  The quadratic opens
downward, so $1/2$ lies strictly between its two roots.  Therefore
$c\le1/2$ selects the smaller-root component containing $c=0$.

For Cell $L$, the fiber from $c=0$ reaches one selected root in
$[\sqrt3/2,1]$; monotonicity there follows from
$4c^3-2c+m>0$.  At $q=0$ the $L$ and $T$ formulas meet and both give $c=s$.
This proves \eqref{eq:derived-admissible-cells} and, in particular, proves that
$\mathcal A$ is coordinatewise down-closed.

\subsection{The radial envelope}

Solving the selected frontier equations gives
\[
 c_{\max}(a,b)=
 \begin{cases}
  1,&a=b=0,\\
  C_L(m),&s\le1,\ q\le0,\ (a,b)\ne(0,0),\\
  \dfrac{2M}{1+\sqrt{4s^2-3}},&s\le1,\ q>0,\\[2mm]
  \dfrac{M(2mM+1)-M\sqrt{4d-3}}{2(1-m^2)},&s>1,
 \end{cases}
\]
where $C_L(m)$ is the unique root in $[\sqrt3/2,1]$ of
\[
 z^4-z^2+mz-m^2=0.
\]
At $q=0$ the two subcritical formulas agree and equal $s$.  These formulas are
the radial-envelope inputs used later in Strategies~2 and~4.
