\section{Strategy 2: Exact Demand Propagation}
\label{sec:strategy2-reader}

This section gives the reusable geometric mechanism and the shortened proofs
that drive Strategy~2.  The exact admissible-set classification, all radical
comparisons, the complete four-label tables, and the unchanged terminal
polynomial estimates are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{sec:exact-local-demand-calculus} and its following Strategy~2
sections.  In the body, every formal map is connected explicitly to an actual
nonsupercritical row.

\subsection{The local propagation interface}

Let
\[
 u=\left(\frac12,\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right),
 \qquad
 v=\left(\frac12,-\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right),
\]
and for $0\le a,b,c\le1$ put
\[
 K(a,b,c)=\conv\{0,au,bv,c(u+v)\}.
\]
The local admissible set is
\[
 \mathcal A
 =\{(a,b,c):K(a,b,c)\text{ lies in a closed unit equilateral triangle}\}.
\]
For $a,c\in[0,1]$ define
\[
 B_c(a)=\max\{b:(a,b,c)\in\mathcal A\},
 \qquad
 F_c(a)=\min\{B_c(a),1-a\},
 \qquad
 G_c(a)=1-F_c(a).
\]
Propositions~\ref{prop:exact-admissible-set} and
\ref{prop:capped-demand-map} prove that $\mathcal A$ is coordinatewise
down-closed, $F_c$ is nonincreasing, and $G_c$ is nondecreasing and
extensive:
\begin{equation}
 G_c(z)\ge z.
 \label{eq:reader-extensive}
\end{equation}
They also prove the exact reflection duality
\begin{equation}
 G_c(a)\le z
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 G_c(1-z)\le1-a.
 \label{eq:reader-capped-duality}
\end{equation}

Suppose an actual row has maximal reaches $(A_i,B_i,C_i)$ and is
nonsupercritical.  If
\[
 A_i\ge z,\qquad C_i\ge c_i,\qquad A_i+B_i\le1,
\]
then the same role realizes the lower-bound demand triple $(z,B_i,c_i)$ and
therefore
\[
 B_i\le F_{c_i}(z).
\]
If the next boundary handoff gives $A_{i+1}\ge1-B_i$, then
\begin{equation}
 A_{i+1}
 \ge1-B_i
 \ge1-F_{c_i}(z)
 =G_{c_i}(z).
 \label{eq:reader-actual-propagation}
\end{equation}
Every composition below is shorthand for repeated applications of
\eqref{eq:reader-actual-propagation} to the stated actual rows.

\subsection{A universal normal form for every selected $T_+$ arc}

The exact capped map has four genuine labels: Full, $L$, $T_-$, and selected
$T_+$.  The nonlinear part of the last label is independent of the radial
deficit.

\begin{proposition}[Universal selected-$T_+$ curve]
\label{prop:reader-universal-tplus}
Fix $0<d<1/2$, put $c=1-d$, and suppose an input $p$ belongs to a selected
$T_+$ arc of $G_c$.  Write
\[
 q=G_c(p),\qquad \nu=q-p,\qquad x=\frac{q-d}{1-d}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 \nu=\psi(x):=1-\sqrt{1-x+x^2},
 \label{eq:reader-psi}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 q=d+(1-d)x,\qquad
 p=d+(1-d)x-\psi(x).
 \label{eq:reader-tplus-normal}
\end{equation}
Consequently $p\mapsto G_{1-d}(p)$ is increasing and strictly concave on
every selected $T_+$ arc.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The selected quadratic in Proposition~\ref{prop:capped-demand-map} is
\[
 (1-q)(q-d)=(1-d)^2\nu(2-\nu).
\]
Substituting $q=d+(1-d)x$ and cancelling $(1-d)^2$ gives
\[
 x(1-x)=\nu(2-\nu).
\]
The selected component uses the smaller root, which is
\eqref{eq:reader-psi}.  Put $r(x)=1-x+x^2$.  Then
\[
 \psi'(x)=\frac{1-2x}{2\sqrt{r(x)}},
 \qquad
 \psi''(x)=-\frac{3}{4r(x)^{3/2}}<0.
\]
The input function
\[
 p_d(x)=d+(1-d)x-\psi(x)
\]
satisfies
\[
 p_d'(x)=1-d-\psi'(x)>0,
 \qquad
 p_d''(x)=-\psi''(x)>0.
\]
Thus $p_d$ is increasing and strictly convex.  Its inverse is increasing and
strictly concave, and $q=d+(1-d)x$ is affine in that inverse.
\end{proof}

Two chord bounds will be used repeatedly.  In the high-radial range put
$e(d)=\ell(1-d)$.  The selected arc has endpoints
\[
 (p,q)=(d,d),\qquad (p,q)=(e(d),d+e(d)),
\]
so
\begin{equation}
 G_{1-d}(p)
 \ge p+\frac{d}{e(d)-d}(p-d).
 \label{eq:reader-high-chord}
\end{equation}
In the low-radial range, if $t=h(1-d)$ is the order-transition value, the
endpoints are $(d,d)$ and $(t,1-t)$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
 G_{1-d}(p)
 \ge p+\frac{1-2t}{t-d}(p-d).
 \label{eq:reader-low-chord}
\end{equation}

For algebraic calculations the same curve has a rational parameter.  With
$z=\psi(x)/x$,
\begin{equation}
 x=\frac{1-2z}{1-z^2},\qquad
 \psi(x)=\frac{z(1-2z)}{1-z^2}.
 \label{eq:reader-tplus-rational}
\end{equation}
In the historical high-sheet notation $x=1-\beta$ and
$m_\beta=\sqrt{\beta^2-\beta+1}$, this becomes
\begin{equation}
 \beta=\frac{z(2-z)}{1-z^2},\qquad
 m_\beta=\frac{1-z+z^2}{1-z^2}.
 \label{eq:reader-beta-rational}
\end{equation}
This is the normalization used in the shortened $407X$ high-sheet proof.

\subsection{Threshold routing}

For $0<d<1-\sqrt3/2$, the exact high-demand threshold is
\begin{equation}
 z>e(d)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 G_{1-d}(z)\ge1-e(d).
 \label{eq:reader-high-threshold}
\end{equation}
The following lemma isolates the only logical use of a long map composition
after such a threshold has been crossed.

\begin{lemma}[Threshold routing]
\label{lem:reader-threshold-routing}
Let $z_j=G_j(z_{j-1})$, where every $G_j$ is nondecreasing and extensive.
If $G_k=G_{1-d}$ and $z_{k-1}>e(d)$, then
\[
 z_j\ge1-e(d)
 \qquad(j\ge k).
\]
In particular, if $e(d)<Q$, then every later output is strictly larger than
$1-Q$.

Suppose instead that a chain contains later occurrences of both
$G_{1-d_1}$ and $G_{1-d_2}$, that
\[
 z_0>e(d_1),\qquad
 \min\{e(d_1),e(d_2)\}<Q.
\]
Then one of the two threshold rows forces every subsequent output above
$1-Q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:reader-high-threshold} gives
$z_k\ge1-e(d)$, and extensivity preserves this bound.  For the two-threshold
statement, if $e(d_1)<Q$, route the initial strict inequality to the
$G_{1-d_1}$ row.  Otherwise
\[
 z_0>e(d_1)\ge Q>e(d_2),
\]
so route it to the $G_{1-d_2}$ row.  The first triggered row gives the desired
strict output, and all later maps preserve it.
\end{proof}

The lemma does not replace the actual-row argument.  It is applied only after
\eqref{eq:reader-actual-propagation} has identified the formal iterates as
lower bounds for the actual incoming reaches.

\subsection{The CE1 one-gap chain with the shortened $X>1/2$ proof}

We give the full logical route and isolate the unchanged terminal estimate in
one appendix lemma.  Use the exact CE1 variables of
Proposition~\ref{prop:center-exact-formulas}:
\[
 R=\lambda,\qquad w=1-R,\qquad
 E=\sqrt{1-R+R^2}=\sqrt{1-Rw},
\]
\[
 \eta=1-E,\qquad P=\eta E,\qquad A=C_0,\qquad D=C_2.
\]
They satisfy
\begin{equation}
 A,D>0,\qquad A+wD<P,\qquad D+RA\ge P,\qquad
 A<\frac w2,\qquad D<\frac R2.
 \label{eq:reader-ce1-domain}
\end{equation}
Put
\[
 X=R-D,\qquad
 m=\frac AR,\qquad
 H=\frac{\eta+A+D}{2R}.
\]
The five high radial demands are
\[
 c_1=1-\frac DR,\quad c_2=1-D,\quad c_3=1-m,\quad
 c_4=1-A,\quad c_5=1-\frac Aw.
\]

A gap in the center interval $[s,t]\subset e_{0,1}$ gives
$a_1\ge X=1-t$ and $b_0\ge s$.  Applying
\eqref{eq:reader-actual-propagation} to rows $1,2,3,4,5$ gives
\begin{equation}
 a_0\ge
 Z:=(G_{c_5}\circ G_{c_4}\circ G_{c_3}\circ G_{c_2}\circ G_{c_1})(X).
 \label{eq:reader-ce1-five-map}
\end{equation}
The supercritical row realizes $(s,a_0,c_0)$ after reflection and
coordinatewise relaxation, so
\begin{equation}
 a_0\le B_{c_0}(s).
 \label{eq:reader-ce1-target}
\end{equation}

Duality and extensivity reduce the lower bound to the three-row suffix
\begin{equation}
 (G_{c_2}\circ G_{c_3}\circ G_{c_4})(H)>1-X.
 \label{eq:reader-ce1-suffix}
\end{equation}
The elementary low-root estimates give
\begin{equation}
 e(A)<X,\qquad H>A.
 \label{eq:reader-ce1-first-bounds}
\end{equation}
For completeness, the first inequality follows from
$A+wD<P$ and $D=R-X$.  Indeed, with
\[
 f(X)=wX-\frac{X}{2(1+X)},
\]
convexity and the endpoint values at $X=R/2,R$ give $f(X)<\eta$, hence
\[
 A<\frac{X}{2(1+X)}.
\]
Together with $e(A)<2A/(1-2A)$ this gives $e(A)<X$.  Also
\[
 2R(H-A)=\eta+D+(1-2R)A>0;
\]
when $R>1/2$, use $A<P=\eta E$ to obtain the strict sign.

At row $4$, the Full labels are impossible.  The $L$ and $T_-$ labels give
\[
 G_{1-A}(H)\ge1-e(A)>1-X.
\]
It remains to consider the selected $T_+$ label.

\begin{lemma}[The selected row-$4$ branch forces $X>1/2$]
\label{lem:reader-ce1-x-half}
Under \eqref{eq:reader-ce1-domain}, if $H\le e(A)$, then
\[
 X>\frac12.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $X\le1/2$.  The selected label and the strict low-root estimate give
\[
 H\le e(A)<\frac{2A}{1-2A}.
\]
The other center inequality gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 2RH
 &=\eta+A+D\\
 &\ge\eta+A+P-RA\\
 &=\eta+P+wA=w(R+A).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Consequently
\[
 f_R(A)<0,\qquad
 f_R(z):=w(R+z)(1-2z)-4Rz.
\]
But $f_R''(z)=-4w<0$ and $f_R(0)=Rw>0$.

If $R\le1/2$, then $0<A<P$.  Using $P=(1-E)E$ and
$E^2=1-R+R^2$,
\[
 f_R(P)
 =(1-E)\left(4E^3-2E^2+1-R(2E^3-2E^2+5E)\right).
\]
The coefficient of $R$ is positive, so
\[
 f_R(P)\ge\frac{1-E}{2}\phi(E),\qquad
 \phi(E)=6E^3-2E^2-5E+2.
\]
On $\sqrt3/2\le E<1$,
\[
 \phi'(E)=18E^2-4E-5>0,\qquad
 \phi(\sqrt3/2)=\frac{2-\sqrt3}{4}>0.
\]
Thus $f_R(P)>0$.  Strict concavity places $f_R$ above the positive chord
joining its values at $0$ and $P$, contradicting $f_R(A)<0$.

If $R>1/2$, then $D=R-X\ge R-1/2$, and the first center inequality gives
\[
 0<A<A_0,\qquad
 A_0=P-w\left(R-\frac12\right)=\frac w2-\eta.
\]
Again using $E^2=1-R+R^2$,
\[
 f_R(A_0)=\frac{1-E}{2}(E+11R-3ER-5).
\]
Since $11-3E>0$ and $R>1/2$,
\[
 E+11R-3ER-5
 >\frac{11-3E}{2}+E-5
 =\frac{1-E}{2}>0.
\]
Thus $f_R(A_0)>0$, and concavity gives the same contradiction on
$(0,A_0)$.  Therefore $X>1/2$.
\end{proof}

Assume now that rows $4$ and $3$ are both selected $T_+$.  Put
\[
 p_1=G_{1-A}(H),\qquad p_2=G_{1-m}(p_1).
\]
The high-radial chord \eqref{eq:reader-high-chord} and the low-root bounds
give
\begin{equation}
 p_1>L_1:=(2-4A)H-(1-4A)A.
 \label{eq:reader-L1}
\end{equation}
Since $X>1/2$, one has $R>1/2$ and $m<w/2<1/4$.  Applying the appropriate
high- or low-radial chord from
\eqref{eq:reader-high-chord}--\eqref{eq:reader-low-chord} gives
\begin{equation}
 p_2>L_2:=(2-5m)L_1-(1-5m)m.
 \label{eq:reader-L2}
\end{equation}
The coefficient comparison is elementary: for $m\le1/8$ use
$e(m)\le2m+5m^2$; for $1/8<m<1-\sqrt3/2$ use
$e(m)<(1-m)/2$; and on the low-radial arc use
$h(1-m)<7/16<(3+m)/7$.

The unchanged terminal calculation in
Appendix~\ref{sec:cyclic-all-vd0} proves from
\eqref{eq:reader-ce1-domain}, the two selected labels, and
\eqref{eq:reader-L2} that
\begin{equation}
 D<\frac1{10},\qquad
 p_2>L_2>2D+3D^2>e(D).
 \label{eq:reader-ce1-terminal}
\end{equation}
Now threshold routing, rather than a new five-label table, finishes the
argument.  The row-$2$ map is $G_{1-D}$, so
\[
 G_{1-D}(p_2)\ge1-e(D)>1-X,
\]
where the last inequality follows from
\[
 e(D)<2D+3D^2<\frac{23}{100}<\frac12<X.
\]
This is \eqref{eq:reader-ce1-suffix}.  Duality and the extensive outer maps
give
\[
 Z>1-H=1-\frac s2.
\]
Finally the boundary diameter condition gives
\[
 B_{c_0}(s)
 \le\frac{-s+\sqrt{4-3s^2}}2
 <1-\frac s2.
\]
Together with \eqref{eq:reader-ce1-five-map} and
\eqref{eq:reader-ce1-target}, this is the contradiction
\[
 a_0\ge Z>B_{c_0}(s)\ge a_0.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Simplified CE1 one-gap obstruction]
\label{prop:reader-ce1-one-gap}
The CE1, $N_+=1$, all-$\Vdzero$, exactly-one-gap state is impossible.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The actual-row induction, the suffix duality, the branch split above, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:reader-ce1-x-half} prove the required terminal contradiction in
every selected row-$4$ label.
\end{proof}

\subsection{CE2 one-gap routing}

The CE2 proof is an especially clean use of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reader-threshold-routing}.  Normalize the exact center
parameters by
\[
 R=\frac{x}{x+y},\qquad w=1-R,\qquad E=\sqrt{1-Rw},\qquad
 \eta=1-E,\qquad P=\eta E,
\]
and put
\[
 \alpha=u-R,\qquad \delta=v-w,\qquad k=\eta+\alpha+\delta.
\]
The strict center domain is
\[
 \alpha,\delta>0,\qquad
 \delta+R\alpha<P,\qquad
 \alpha+w\delta<P.
\]
For a right-hand gap the initial input is
\[
 z_0=1-v=R-\delta,
\]
and the decisive target is
\[
 Q=\frac y2=\frac{k}{2R}.
\]
The exact center inequalities, proved in
Appendix~\ref{sec:cyclic-all-vd0}, give
\begin{equation}
 z_0>e(\alpha),\qquad
 \min\{e(\alpha),e(\delta)\}<Q.
 \label{eq:reader-ce2-two-thresholds}
\end{equation}
The five actual nonsupercritical rows give the formal chain by
\eqref{eq:reader-actual-propagation}.  If $e(\alpha)<Q$, the row with radial
deficit $\alpha$ forces the output above $1-Q$.  Otherwise
\[
 z_0>e(\alpha)\ge Q>e(\delta),
\]
and the row with deficit $\delta$ does so.  Every subsequent map is extensive.
Thus the return demand satisfies
\[
 Z_{\mathrm{CE2}}^R>1-\frac y2.
\]
The diameter endpoint gives
\[
 B_{c_0}(y)<1-\frac y2,
\]
contradicting the two actual bounds on row $0$.  Reflection exchanges
$(x,u,\alpha,R)$ with $(y,v,\delta,w)$ and proves the left-gap orientation.
This is Proposition~\ref{prop:ce2-one-gap}.

\subsection{The $407X$ high-sheet package}

The T3-like $N_+=0$ branch reduces to one strict two-coordinate capped-loss
inequality.  Its difficult first-coordinate label is the selected high sheet.
The local radical formerly written
\[
 m_\beta=\sqrt{\beta^2-\beta+1}
\]
is exactly the universal curve
\eqref{eq:reader-psi} with $x=1-\beta$, and may be replaced by the rational
coordinate \eqref{eq:reader-beta-rational}.  The center radical
$\rho=\sqrt{r^2-r+1}$ is independent and is retained.

The exact high-sheet estimates in
Appendix~\ref{sec:t3-vd-demand-branches} prove
\[
 y_*<\frac1{10},\qquad 3y_*\le1-B_5,\qquad
 S>3y,\qquad A_C>3y,
\]
and the analytic left-high threshold needed for the right $T_-$ label.  With
the universal normal form, these are the only branch-specific estimates: no
separate selected-$T_+$ curvature calculation remains.  They assemble the
four genuine labels into Proposition~\ref{prop:nplus-zero-t3}.

\subsection{Strategy 2 branch assembly}

\begin{proposition}[Branches closed by exact demand]
\label{prop:reader-demand-branches}
Strategy~2 closes all routing-table rows assigned to exact demand propagation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the $N_+=0$ all-$\Vdzero$ and T3-like-without-Vd1/Vd2 branches for
      $\CEone$ and $\CEtwo$;
\item every positive-gap $N_+=1$ all-$\Vdzero$ state;
\item the exactly-one-T3-like $N_+=1$ state with no Vd1/Vd2 role;
\item the complementary exact placements in the $\CEtwo$, $N_+=1$,
      exactly-one-Vd1/Vd2 branch.
\end{enumerate}
Singleton gaps are included.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first item is Propositions~\ref{prop:nplus-zero-all-vd0} and
\ref{prop:nplus-zero-t3}.  For the second item, the CE1 one-gap state is
Proposition~\ref{prop:reader-ce1-one-gap}; the two CE2 one-gap orientations
are Proposition~\ref{prop:ce2-one-gap}; the CE2 two-gap and all remaining gap
states are assembled in Proposition~\ref{prop:nplus-one-all-vd0}.  The third
item is Proposition~\ref{prop:nplus-one-one-t3}.  The fourth is
Proposition~\ref{prop:ce2-one-vd-placement}.  In every positive-gap chain,
Equation~\eqref{eq:reader-actual-propagation} supplies the passage from scalar
maps to actual role reaches.
\end{proof}
