\section{Finite-Enclosure Obstructions}
\label{sec:finite-enclosure}

The third strategy associates a finite set to the portion of the hexagon not
covered by the six V triangles.  Under a hypothetical cover this set is forced
into the open C triangle.  The contradiction is that its least enclosing
equilateral triangle has side at least one.  The zero-gap all-$\Vdzero$ case
admits the explicit nine-point set constructed later in this section.  For the
nonzero-gap branches, the exact local reach calculations certify the same
noncontainment statement for the finite residual hull.

\subsection{The finite residual hull}

For a compact plane set $K$, let $\Lambda(K)$ be the least side length of a closed equilateral triangle containing $K$.  For fixed open V roles $U_0,\ldots,U_5$, put
\[
 R_V=H\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^5U_i
\]
and let
\[
 K_V=\mathrm{vert}(\conv R_V)
\]
be the vertex set of the convex hull of the residual.  Since the six roles and
$H$ are polygonal, $R_V$ is a compact polygonal set and $K_V$ is finite.

\begin{proposition}[Residual-hull enclosure principle]
\label{prop:residual-hull-enclosure}
Suppose a specified class of open unit C triangles cannot complete the fixed
V roles to a cover of $H$, and every open unit triangle containing $R_V$ is in
that excluded class.  Then
\[
 \Lambda(K_V)\ge1.
\]
Under a hypothetical cover, one also has $K_V\subset U_C$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A cover gives $R_V\subset U_C$, and therefore $K_V\subset U_C$.  Conversely,
suppose $\Lambda(K_V)<1$.  A closed equilateral triangle $T$ of side below
one contains $K_V$, hence contains
\[
 R_V\subseteq\conv K_V\subseteq T.
\]
Enlarge $T$ homothetically to an open unit equilateral triangle $U_C'$.  Then
$R_V\subset U_C'$, so $U_C',U_0,\ldots,U_5$ cover $H$, contrary to the
specified exclusion theorem.
\end{proof}

The point $O$ belongs to $R_V$: every $U_i$ contains $V_i$, while
$\lVert O-V_i\rVert=1$ and two points of one open unit equilateral triangle
have distance strictly below one.  If the V roles leave a boundary gap, then
an open unit triangle containing $R_V$ contains a relative interval of
$\partial H$.  It is therefore CE1 or CE2, including when the V gap is a
singleton.  Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-hull-enclosure} consequently turns
every proved nonzero-gap CE1/CE2 exclusion into a finite-enclosure theorem.

\subsection{Nonzero-gap finite-enclosure terminals}

\begin{proposition}[Former reach-propagation branches]
\label{prop:finite-enclosure-nonzero-gap-branches}
Every nonzero-gap row formerly assigned to boundary-reach propagation is a
finite-enclosure contradiction.  More precisely, the corresponding residual
hull $K_V$ has $\Lambda(K_V)\ge1$ in each of the following cases:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $N_+=0$, all V triangles $\Vdzero$;
 \item $N_+=0$, one or two $\Tlike$ triangles and no $\Vdone/\Vdtwo$ triangle;
 \item $N_+=1$, all V triangles $\Vdzero$;
 \item $N_+=1$, exactly one $\Tlike$ triangle and no $\Vdone/\Vdtwo$ triangle;
 \item $N_+=1$, exactly one $\Vdone/\Vdtwo$ triangle in the surviving CE2
 placement assembly.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:residual-hull-enclosure}.  The exact exclusion
certificates are respectively
Propositions~\ref{prop:nplus-zero-all-vd0},
\ref{prop:nplus-zero-t3},
\ref{prop:nplus-one-all-vd0},
\ref{prop:nplus-one-one-t3}, and
\ref{prop:paper-ce2-one-vd-placements}.  In the last item, a hypothetical
smaller residual-enclosing C triangle that is CE1 is already excluded by the
trace-length branch in Proposition~\ref{prop:length-branches}; the CE2 case is
excluded by Proposition~\ref{prop:paper-ce2-one-vd-placements}.  A boundary
gap excludes CE0 in every item.
\end{proof}
