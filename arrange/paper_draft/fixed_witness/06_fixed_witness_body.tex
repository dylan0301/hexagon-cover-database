\subsection{Fixed witnesses and the case register}
\label{subsec:trace-exact-ab-envelopes}

An obstruction for the original C triangle is not, by itself, an enclosure
inequality. We therefore separate two assertions: the original cover forces
one fixed finite set into $U_C$, and every open unit equilateral candidate
fails to contain that same set. Candidate center parameters are recomputed
in the second assertion; the witness points are never recomputed.

The actual gap on $e_{i,i+1}$ is
\[
 J_i=X_i([B_i,1-A_{i+1}]).
\]
Both endpoints are missed by the incident open V triangles and by all
nonincident V triangles. Equality $B_i=1-A_{i+1}$ is a singleton gap.
Convexity fills a gap from its endpoints, so a candidate containing the
endpoints of all actual gaps completes the boundary with the original
six V triangles. This observation does not assert that it completes the
whole skeleton.

\begin{definition}[Fixed total radial endpoint]
\label{def:fixed-total-radial-endpoint}
Parametrize $r_i$ by $Z_i(c)=(1-c)V_i$, measured from $V_i$ toward $O$.
Let $u_{j\to i}$ be the largest parameter in the actual positive trace of
$T_j$ on $r_i$ for $j=i-1,i+1$, with value zero when no such positive trace
exists. Put
\[
 \gamma_i:=\max\{C_i,u_{i-1\to i},u_{i+1\to i}\},\qquad
 d_i:=1-\gamma_i,\qquad \widehat P_i:=d_iV_i.
\]
\end{definition}

Thus $\widehat P_i$ is the innermost endpoint reached by any local V
triangle on $r_i$, rather than just the endpoint of the own V triangle.
This distinction is essential when a nonsupercritical V triangle supports
an adjacent radial segment.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed radial forcing and demand recovery]
\label{lem:fixed-total-radial-forcing}
One has $0<d_i<1$ and $\widehat P_i\notin\bigcup_jU_j$. Under skeleton
coverage, $\widehat P_i\in U_C$. If an arbitrary open unit candidate $U'$
contains $O$ and $\widehat P_i$, and its exit on $r_i$ is $d_i'$ measured
from $O$, then $\gamma_i>1-d_i'$. If both neighboring endpoints are at most
$1-d_i'$, it follows that
\[
 C_i>1-d_i'.
\]
It is sufficient that each neighboring V triangle be nonsupercritical with
both actual boundary reaches greater than $d_i'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A local open triangle containing the total endpoint would extend its trace
farther toward $O$. Nonlocal triangles are excluded by diameter. The last
assertion follows from common-pair domination, which bounds a neighboring
endpoint by $1-\min\{A_j,B_j\}$. The full endpoint and strictness argument
is in \zcref{lem:appendix-fixed-total-endpoint}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The origin is implicit in the path witnesses]
\label{lem:fixed-origin-in-hull}
For $G=X_0(t)$, $0<t<1$, and $d_2,d_4>0$,
\[
 G+\frac{1-t}{d_2}(d_2V_2)+\frac1{d_4}(d_4V_4)=0.
\]
Consequently $O\in\conv\{G,d_2V_2,d_4V_4\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use $V_2=V_1-V_0$ and $V_4=-V_1$, and normalize the three positive
coefficients to sum to one.
\end{proof}

There are five witness recipes, but only three principal mechanisms:
a nonsupercritical boundary path, a supported rescuer, and the zero-gap
asymmetric construction. The complementary-gap disk is a shorter alternative
for the all-nonsupercritical one-gap case. In the nonzero-gap rows normalize
the C triangle's distinguished radial midpoint to $M_0$. When $N_+=1$, let
$\sigma$ be the unique supercritical index. All point counts below are upper
bounds; coincident gap endpoints can reduce them.

\subsection{A: the complementary-gap disk}

\begin{lemma}[One-gap common-pair adapter]
\label{lem:new-one-gap-common-pair-adapter}
Suppose $N_+=0$ and the unique actual gap is
$J_0=X_0([B_0,1-A_1])$. Put $p=A_1$, $q=B_0$. Then $p,q>0$,
$p+q\le1$, and $A_i\ge p$, $B_i\ge q$ for all $i$, without a V-type
restriction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The five gap-free edges give $B_i+A_{i+1}>1$, while
$A_i+B_i\le1$. Thus
\[
 A_1<A_2<A_3<A_4<A_5<A_0,\qquad
 B_0<B_5<B_4<B_3<B_2<B_1.
\]
The actual gap gives $p+q\le1$. These inequalities prove the assertion.
\end{proof}

Put
\[
 c_A:=c_{\max}(A_1,B_0),\qquad D_i^A:=(1-c_A)V_i,
\]
\[
 G_A:=
 \begin{cases}X_0(B_0),& B_0\le A_1,\\
 X_0(1-A_1),& A_1<B_0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad K_A:=\{D_0^A,\ldots,D_5^A,G_A\}.
\]
The disk used in this recipe is exactly
\[
 \mathcal D_A:=\left\{x\in\mathbb R^2:
 \|x\|\le\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\bigl(1-c_{\max}(A_1,B_0)\bigr)\right\}.
\]
It is the inscribed disk of the regular hexagon formed by the six radial
points. It need not itself be missed pointwise by the V triangles: its
containment in $U_C$ follows from convexity of the six forced points.

\begin{theorem}[Complementary-gap finite enclosure]
\label{thm:new-complementary-gap}
For $p,q>0$, $p+q\le1$, $c_*=c_{\max}(p,q)$, and
$J(p,q)=X_0([q,1-p])$, let $G$ be its farther endpoint from $O$. Then
\[
 \Lambda\bigl(\mathbb B(O,h(1-c_*))\cup\{G\}\bigr)\ge1.
\]
Consequently the all-nonsupercritical one-gap configuration forces
$K_A\subset U_C$ and satisfies $\Lambda(K_A)\ge1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The disk--point calculation in
\zcref{lem:new-disk-point-formula,lem:appendix-complementary-gap}
uses only the farther endpoint of the actual gap. Common-pair forcing puts
the six $D_i^A$ in $U_C$, and their hull contains the stated disk. The gap
endpoint is center-forced. Monotonicity of $\Lambda$ under convex hulls
therefore proves the finite-set assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{B and C: a nonsupercritical-path enclosure theorem}

In B the gaps are on $e_{5,0},e_{0,1}$; in C the single gap is on
$e_{0,1}$. In both clauses the path consists of $T_1,\ldots,T_5$ and
satisfies $A_i+B_i\le1$. The role $T_0$ may itself be supercritical or
nonsupercritical. Every witness is computed from the original V triangles.

\begin{lemma}[Two-gap common-pair adapter for a candidate]
\label{lem:new-two-gap-common-pair-adapter}
Let an open unit candidate $U'$ contain
$X_5(B_5),X_0(1-A_1),\widehat P_2,\widehat P_4$. Then $O\in U'$ and the
candidate is CE2 with common midpoint $M_0$. For its own signed variables
put $p=W-\alpha$, $q=R-\delta$. In the two-gap path configuration,
\[
 A_i>q,\qquad B_i>p\qquad(1\le i\le5).
\]
Thus every own or permitted neighboring contribution on $r_2,r_4$ is at
most $c_{\max}(p,q)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use \zcref{lem:fixed-origin-in-hull}. The two boundary anchors force positive
traces on both adjacent edges, hence CE2. Their strict containment gives
$B_5>p$, $A_1>q$. The four gap-free edges propagate these inequalities
along the nonsupercritical path. Common-pair domination also bounds both
possible neighboring contributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Four-point two-gap enclosure]
\label{thm:new-ce2-short-ray}
Under the B path hypotheses, the fixed set
\[
 K_B:=\{X_5(B_5),X_0(1-A_1),\widehat P_2,\widehat P_4\}
\]
satisfies $\Lambda(K_B)\ge1$. Skeleton coverage forces $K_B\subset U_C$.
There is no disk in this recipe.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forcing uses actual gap endpoints and total radial endpoints. For any
candidate containing $K_B$, the preceding adapter and the signed CE2
inequality $c_{\max}(p,q)<1-\min\{\alpha,\delta\}$ place
$\widehat P_2$ beyond exit $\delta$ or $\widehat P_4$ beyond exit $\alpha$.
The full arbitrary-candidate argument is
\zcref{lem:appendix-fixed-two-gap}. Neither point is recomputed from the
candidate center.
\end{proof}

For C define
\[
 K_C:=\{M_0,X_0(B_0),X_0(1-A_1),
             \widehat P_2,\widehat P_3,\widehat P_4\}.
\]
The midpoint is a structural anchor belonging to the original open C
triangle. The other points are missed by the V triangles. The origin is
implicit by \zcref{lem:fixed-origin-in-hull}; the construction uses at most
six points even though a candidate must also contain $O$.

\begin{lemma}[The fixed transverse witness is center-forced]
\label{lem:readable-k410-forced}
In the C placement, with the original C triangle's distinguished midpoint
$M_0$, skeleton coverage implies $K_C\subset U_C$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use the two actual gap endpoints, total radial forcing on $r_2,r_3,r_4$,
and the distinguished midpoint. No Vd0 locality is assumed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[CE2 transverse threshold terminal]
\label{prop:readable-k410-ce2}
No open unit CE2 candidate contains $K_C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The fixed midpoint and implicit origin normalize every candidate. Its
boundary witnesses give a common path pair $(X,Q)$, where
$X=R-\delta$ and $Q=(\eta+\alpha+\delta)/(2R)$. The two thresholds in
\zcref{lem:appendix-fixed-transverse} exclude the total endpoint on $r_2$
or $r_4$ using common-pair domination.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[CE1 transverse return with neighboring support]
\label{prop:readable-k410-ce1}
No open unit CE1 candidate contains $K_C$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the candidate's signed domain, both boundary reaches of every relevant
path role exceed $\min\{X,Q\}>\alpha/R>\alpha$. Neighbor estimates and
fixed-endpoint containment first recover the own demands at $T_4,T_3$.
The first selected return step gives $B_3>L_1$, and the positive identity
\[
 R(L_1-\delta)
 =(1-2\alpha)\eta+(W-2\alpha)(\alpha+\delta)+4R\alpha^2
\]
excludes both neighboring contributions on $r_2$. This recovers the third
own demand before the full return is invoked. The exact argument is
\zcref{lem:appendix-fixed-transverse}; the conditional local return remains
\zcref{prop:new-ce1-direct-certificate}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Six-point type-independent transverse enclosure]
\label{prop:new-nplus-one-all-vd0}
Under the C path hypotheses, $\Lambda(K_C)\ge1$. The theorem imposes no
V-type restriction on $T_1,\ldots,T_5$ and does not require $T_0$ itself
to be supercritical. No disk is used.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every candidate contains $O,M_0$ and a boundary point openly, hence is CE1
or CE2. The preceding propositions exclude both classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Every skeleton cover has a supercritical V triangle]
\label{prop:new-nplus-zero-gap-closures}
\label{thm:fixed-nzero}
A skeleton cover by the seven original open roles satisfies $N_+\ge1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $N_+=0$ and there are no gaps, strict overlaps give
$6<\sum_i(A_i+B_i)\le6$. With one gap use A. With two gaps use B. The
C-triangle boundary classification makes these alternatives exhaustive.
\end{proof}

\subsection{D: a four-point supported-rescuer enclosure}

Let $Y(t)=(1-t)V_0+tV_5=X_5(1-t)$. The geometric lemma below is stated
independently of any V type or covering configuration.

\begin{theorem}[Four-point rescuer geometry]
\label{thm:fixed-four-point-rescuer}
Suppose $a\ge0$, $\varepsilon>0$, $\beta\ge0$, and
\[
 s=a+\varepsilon\le1,\qquad a\le\varepsilon,\qquad
 \beta\le\frac{\varepsilon}{s}.
\]
Then
\[
 \Lambda\{O,\varepsilon V_1,Y(a),Y(1-\beta)\}\ge1.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The convex combination
\[
 \frac{\varepsilon}{s}Y(a)+\frac a s(\varepsilon V_1)
 =\frac{\varepsilon}{s}V_0
\]
forces $M_0$ into any candidate containing the four points. Its left boundary
trace and exit on $r_1$ then give the incompatible inequalities
$1-\beta<R+\alpha<a/s\le1-\beta$.
The reflected one-edge normal form and the endpoint cases are checked in
\zcref{lem:appendix-fixed-four-point}.
\end{proof}

For the two actual adapters normalize $T_0$ as the rescuer of $M_1$,
$T_1$ as uniquely supercritical, and $T_2,\ldots,T_5$ as nonsupercritical
ordinary roles on the four center-free edges. Write
\[
 T_0\cap r_1=\{(1-t)V_1:c\le t\le u\},\qquad
 0<c\le\tfrac12\le u<1,\qquad \varepsilon=1-u.
\]
Let $M=M_c^{\rm sup}=(c+\sqrt{c^2-8c+4})/2$, with the value $1/2$ at
$c=1/2$ understood as the continuous endpoint convention. The local
requirements are
\[
 \varepsilon V_1\in U_C,\quad C_1\ge c,\quad
 A_0+\varepsilon\le1,\quad
 \frac{A_0}{A_0+\varepsilon}\le1-M.
\]
The separate inequality $A_0\le1-M$ follows from the last two and is not
an additional hypothesis.

The strict-supercritical envelope gives $B_1<M$; the four ordinary handoffs
give $B_5\le B_1$. Thus $B_5<1-A_0$, and the two left-gap endpoints are
$Y(A_0)$ and $Y(1-B_5)$. The fixed set is
\[
 \boxed{K_D=\{O,\varepsilon V_1,Y(A_0),Y(1-B_5)\}.}
\]
It lies in $U_C$ and satisfies the four-point theorem with geometric
parameters $a=A_0$, $\beta=B_5$. No sum greater than four is needed.
There is no disk, and no distinction between one and two actual gaps in
this enclosure step.

\paragraph{T3-like local input.}
In its translated chart put
\[
 1\le D_T\le2/\sqrt3,\qquad
 R_T=(D_T+\sqrt{4-3D_T^2})/2.
\]
The actual supported endpoints are
\[
 c=\frac{D_T(1+A_0)-1}{R_T},\qquad
 u=1-\frac{R_T}{D_T}+A_0,\qquad
 \varepsilon=\frac{R_T}{D_T}-A_0.
\]
The T3-like chart calculation verifies the local requirements.

\paragraph{Vd1 local input.}
With $t\ge1$ and $d_t=\sqrt{t^2+t+1}$, the actual corner data give
\[
 c=\frac{t(1-B_0)}{t+1},\qquad
 u=\frac{d_t-A_0-tB_0-1}{t},\qquad
 \varepsilon=\frac{t+1-d_t+A_0+tB_0}{t}.
\]
The Vd1 chart calculation verifies the same requirements. These two chart
calculations remain in Appendix D; their global ending is identical.

\begin{proposition}[T3-like supported-rescuer terminal]
\label{prop:new-one-t3-terminal}
Suppose $N_+=1$, there is exactly one T3-like role and no Vd1/Vd2 role,
and there is at least one actual gap. Then the skeleton is not covered.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Midpoint reduction places the T3-like rescuer at $T_0$ and the supercritical
role at $T_1$, after reflection. The local calculation of
\zcref{lem:appendix-t3-supported-tail} supplies the supported endpoint and
ratio bound. Apply the four-point theorem to $K_D$ for either gap rank.
No common pair is propagated through the supercritical $T_1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The remaining placement router}

\begin{proposition}[One-Vd placement assembly]
\label{prop:new-one-vd-assembly}
Suppose the C triangle is CE2, $N_+=1$, exactly one V triangle is Vd1 or
Vd2, and there is at least one actual gap. Then the skeleton is not covered.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An additional positive-support role closes by the skeleton-length filter.
Otherwise let $\tau$ be the Vd index, $\sigma$ the supercritical index,
and normalize the center's midpoint to $M_0$.
If $\sigma=0$, use C with one gap or B with two gaps. No distinction
between $\tau\in\{1,5\}$ and $\tau\in\{2,3,4\}$ remains in the active
route; the old residual estimates are alternative local calculations.

If $\tau=0$, midpoint rescue forces $\sigma\in\{1,5\}$. A Vd2 rescuer
uses the retained perimeter bound. For Vd1, reflect to $\sigma=1$; the
local Vd1 endpoint calculation and the four-point D theorem apply to both
gap ranks.

If $\sigma,\tau\ne0$, midpoint rescue makes the two special roles
adjacent. Vd2 again closes by length. For Vd1, make a fresh cyclic
renumbering with $(\tau,\sigma)=(0,1)$. The reduced replacement hypotheses
are
\[
 T_0\text{ Vd1},\quad M_1\in T_0,\quad A_1+B_1>1,
 \quad A_i+B_i\le1\ (i\ne1),
\]
\[
 T_i\text{ Vd0}\ (1\le i\le5),\qquad
 U_C\cap e_{0,1}=\varnothing,
\]
\[
 \Haus(T_j\cap r_k)=0\qquad(2\le j\le5,\ k\in\{0,1\}).
\]
The two-chart replacement of
\zcref{prop:appendix-vd1-two-chart-replacement} keeps the original C
triangle fixed, preserves the skeleton, and produces six V triangles with
actual boundary sums at most one. It is therefore contradicted directly
by \zcref{thm:fixed-nzero}. The output gap rank need not equal the input
rank; the zero/one/two-gap split is already internal to that theorem.
The separate vertex charts and all strict replacement margins are retained.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[All nonzero-gap finite-enclosure rows]
\label{prop:finite-enclosure-nonzero-gap-branches}
Every nonzero-gap row of \zcref{tab:routing} assigned to finite enclosure
is impossible.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $N_+=0$ use \zcref{thm:fixed-nzero}. If the unique supercritical role
is based at the center midpoint, use C or B by gap rank. The reduced
center-based T3-like and Vd1 rescuers use D. The remaining away Vd1 pair
uses replacement followed by \zcref{thm:fixed-nzero}. The structural
placement audit and the explicit length exits exhaust the remaining rows.
\end{proof}
