\subsection{Fixed endpoints and arbitrary candidates}

The new enclosure theorems use actual trace endpoints, fixed before an
arbitrary candidate is chosen. This subsection supplies the new arguments;
the subsequent local capacity and selected-return calculations are retained.
The BC tail bound belongs to the original configuration; candidate
parameters do not alter its six witness points. The optional B comparison
pair likewise belongs to the candidate, while its four witnesses stay fixed.

\begin{lemma}[Total radial endpoint and maximum-to-own-demand]
\label{lem:appendix-fixed-total-endpoint}
The assertions of \zcref{lem:fixed-total-radial-forcing} hold for arbitrary
V types.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
No closed V triangle contains $O$: its distinguished vertex is interior
and at distance one from $O$, so moving that vertex slightly away from $O$
inside the open triangle would contradict diameter one. Each own trace has
positive length. Hence $0<\gamma_i<1$ and $0<d_i<1$.

If a local open role contained $Z_i(\gamma_i)$, openness would give a point
$Z_i(c)$ with $c>\gamma_i$ in that role. A role with no positive neighboring
trace would acquire one; otherwise its maximal endpoint would increase.
Both contradict the definition of $\gamma_i$. The nonlocal roles are
excluded by
\[
 \|d_iV_i-V_{i\pm2}\|^2=1+d_i+d_i^2>1,
 \qquad \|d_iV_i-V_{i+3}\|=1+d_i>1.
\]
Thus every total endpoint is missed by all open V roles. For
$\gamma_i\le\Gamma<1$, the same proof with $d=1-\Gamma$ excludes
$(1-\Gamma)V_i$: openness would give a local endpoint strictly above
$\Gamma\ge\gamma_i$. For $\Gamma=1$, the point is $O$, which was already
excluded even from the closed V triangles.

Now let an arbitrary open unit candidate contain $O$ and $\widehat P_i$.
If its radial exit is $d_i'$, open containment gives $d_i<d_i'$ and hence
$\gamma_i>1-d_i'$. If both neighboring endpoints are at most $1-d_i'$,
the own term must realize this strict demand. For a nonsupercritical
neighbor with both actual boundary reaches above $d_i'$, the bound
$C_\pm(A_j,B_j)\le1-\min\{A_j,B_j\}<1-d_i'$ excludes its contribution.
No skeleton-cover hypothesis for this new candidate is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed four-point two-gap enclosure]
\label{lem:appendix-fixed-two-gap}
The fixed set $K_B$ in \zcref{thm:new-ce2-short-ray} has
$\Lambda(K_B)\ge1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose an open unit candidate $U'$ contains $K_B$. The origin-hull identity
puts $O$ in it, and its two boundary anchors give positive traces on both
$e_{5,0}$ and $e_{0,1}$. Thus the candidate is CE2 in the common-vertex
normalization. Recompute $R,W,E,\eta,P,\alpha,\delta$ from this candidate
and put
\[
 p=W-\alpha,\qquad q=R-\delta,\qquad c_*=c_{\max}(p,q).
\]
Strict containment of the two anchors yields $B_5>p$, $A_1>q$.
The four intervening gap-free edges and nonsupercriticality give
\[
 A_i>q,\qquad B_i>p\qquad(1\le i\le5).
\]
Every local contributor on $r_2$ or $r_4$ belongs to this path, and common-pair
comparison gives $\gamma_2,\gamma_4\le c_*$. The exact signed CE2
inequality proved below in \zcref{lem:appendix-ce2-short-ray} is
\[
 c_*<1-\min\{\alpha,\delta\}.
\]
If $\delta\le\alpha$, then $\|\widehat P_2\|>\delta$, although the
candidate exit on $r_2$ is $\delta$. Otherwise $\|\widehat P_4\|>\alpha$
contradicts its exit on $r_4$. The same fixed points exclude every candidate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed six-point transverse enclosure]
\label{lem:appendix-fixed-transverse}
The set $K_{BC}$ in \zcref{prop:new-nplus-one-all-vd0} has
$\Lambda(K_{BC})\ge1$ for arbitrary V types on the nonsupercritical path.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose an open unit candidate $U'$ contains $K_{BC}$. Its origin is implicit
in the witness hull, and $M_0$ fixes the unique-midpoint normalization.
Write its signed parameters as $R,W,E,\eta,P,\alpha,\delta$ and put
\[
 X=R-\delta,\qquad Q=\frac{\eta+\alpha+\delta}{2R}.
\]
The two selected gap endpoints give $B_0>2Q$ and $A_1>X$.
The explicit tail premise gives $B_5\ge B_0/2>Q$.
The four middle gap-free edges and nonsupercriticality imply
\begin{equation}
 A_1<A_2<A_3<A_4<A_5,\qquad
 A_i>X,\quad B_i>Q\quad(1\le i\le5).
\label{eq:fixed-path-boundary}
\end{equation}
The pair $(X,Q)$ is positive and has sum below one because it is strictly
dominated by a nonsupercritical actual pair.

\emph{CE2 candidates.} Use the selected high-radial root
\[
 e(d)=\frac{1-d}{2}\left(1-\sqrt{4(1-d)^2-3}\right).
\]
The strict CE2 center inequalities give
\begin{equation}
 X>e(\alpha),\qquad \min\{e(\alpha),e(\delta)\}<Q.
\label{eq:fixed-ce2-thresholds}
\end{equation}
For completeness, $WX>\eta+\alpha$, and testing the low-root equation at
this bound reduces the first assertion to
\[
 \pi(t)=(\eta+t)(1-2t)-2Wt>0\qquad(0\le t\le P).
\]
The polynomial is concave, $\pi(0)=\eta>0$, and
$\pi(P)=\eta(\eta+2ER^2)>0$. For the second assertion put
$T=\alpha+\delta$. The weighted sum of the two strict center inequalities
gives $T<\eta/E$. With $d=\min\{\alpha,\delta\}$,
\[
 \min\{e(\alpha),e(\delta)\}
 <\frac{2d}{1-2d}\le\frac{T}{1-T}<Q.
\]
The final comparison follows from concavity of
$\chi(T)=(\eta+T)(1-T)-2RT$, positive at zero and at $\eta/E$, where it
has value $\eta(E-R)^2/E^2$.

If $e(\alpha)<Q$, both coordinates of $(X,Q)$ exceed $e(\alpha)$.
The high-radial threshold therefore gives
$c_{\max}(X,Q)<1-\alpha$. Otherwise
$e(\delta)<Q\le e(\alpha)<X$ and it gives
$c_{\max}(X,Q)<1-\delta$. Common-pair domination and \zcref{eq:fixed-path-boundary} bound all local
contributors on $r_4$ in the first case and $r_2$ in the second. Thus the
candidate misses the corresponding fixed total endpoint.

\emph{CE1 candidates.} Now $R\alpha+\delta\ge P$ and
$\alpha+W\delta<P$. Set $m=\alpha/R$; the transverse exits are
$\alpha,m,\delta$ on $r_4,r_3,r_2$. Combining the two signed inequalities
gives $E^2\alpha<RP$, so $m<\eta/E$. Also
\[
 X>\frac{R}{1+E}>\frac{\eta}{E}>m,
 \qquad 2R(Q-m)=\eta+\delta-\alpha>0.
\]
Here $E>W$ proves the middle inequality, and $\alpha<P=E\eta<\eta$
proves the last sign. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \min\{X,Q\}>m>\alpha.
\label{eq:fixed-ce1-neighbor-floor}
\end{equation}
The neighboring capacities on $r_4,r_3$ are therefore below the candidate's
required demands. Fixed-endpoint containment and
\zcref{lem:appendix-fixed-total-endpoint} recover
\begin{equation}
 C_4>1-\alpha,\qquad C_3>1-m.
\label{eq:fixed-ce1-first-demands}
\end{equation}

Invoke only the first $T_4$ step of the return proof, through
\zcref{eq:ce1-first-chord}. Its tail floor is $B_4>Q$, supplied by
\zcref{eq:fixed-path-boundary}; it uses $C_4>1-\alpha$, but neither
the radial hypothesis at $T_2$ nor that at $T_3$. A nonselected branch
already contradicts the boundary inequalities at $T_1$. On the surviving
selected branch,
\[
 B_3>L_1,\qquad L_1=(2-4\alpha)Q-(1-4\alpha)\alpha.
\]
The identity
\begin{equation}
 R(L_1-\delta)
 =(1-2\alpha)\eta+(W-2\alpha)(\alpha+\delta)+4R\alpha^2>0
\label{eq:fixed-ce1-bridge}
\end{equation}
follows by expansion, with every sign justified by
$0<\alpha<P<\min\{1/2,W/2\}$. Hence $B_3>\delta$.
Backward path monotonicity gives $B_1>B_3>\delta$, and
$A_1,A_3>X>\delta$. Both neighbors of $r_2$ have capacities below
$1-\delta$. Candidate containment now recovers
\begin{equation}
 C_2>1-\delta.
\label{eq:fixed-ce1-third-demand}
\end{equation}
All three own-radial hypotheses of
\zcref{prop:new-ce1-direct-certificate} have been established before the
full tail-input obstruction is used. It gives $B_1>1-X$, contradicting
$A_1>X$ and nonsupercriticality. Its selected-component conditions remain
in force; in particular,
$\delta<1/10$ is not asserted on the bare CE1 signed domain. Both candidate
classes are excluded.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed four-point supported-rescuer proof]
\label{lem:appendix-fixed-four-point}
The geometric statement of \zcref{thm:fixed-four-point-rescuer} holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $a=0$ or $\beta=0$, the set contains $O$ and a hexagon vertex at distance
one, so it cannot lie in an open unit equilateral triangle. Otherwise
suppose a candidate contains the four points. The convex combination in
the theorem's body proof puts $(\varepsilon/s)V_0$ in it, and
$\varepsilon/s\ge1/2$ puts $M_0$ in it by convexity with $O$.
The candidate has a positive boundary trace and unique midpoint $M_0$.
Use the left-active reflected signed normal form when needed; a positive
right-edge trace is not an extra hypothesis. The left trace has endpoints
$k/W,R+\alpha$, and the exit on $r_1$ is $\delta/R$, with
$k=\eta+\alpha+\delta$ and $\eta,\alpha,\delta>0$.
Containment of $Y(a)$ and $\varepsilon V_1$ gives
\[
 Wa>k,\qquad \delta>R\varepsilon,
\]
so $\alpha<a-Rs$ and $R<a/s$. Since $s\le1$,
\[
 R+\alpha<a+R(1-s)\le a/s.
\]
Containment of $Y(1-\beta)$ would require $1-\beta<R+\alpha$, whereas
$\beta\le\varepsilon/s$ gives $1-\beta\ge a/s$. This is impossible.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Using the geometric lemma on an actual supported trace.}
Set the lemma's parameters to $a=A_0$, $\beta=B_5$ and
$\varepsilon=1-u$. The local T3-like or Vd1 certificate gives
\[
 A_0+\varepsilon\le1,
 \qquad \frac{A_0}{A_0+\varepsilon}\le1-M_c^{\rm sup}.
\]
The strict-supercritical envelope and path monotonicity give
$B_5\le B_1<M_c^{\rm sup}$. Since $M_c^{\rm sup}\ge1/2$, these imply
$A_0\le\varepsilon$ and $B_5<\varepsilon/(A_0+\varepsilon)$.
They also imply $A_0\le1-M_c^{\rm sup}$ and hence $B_5<1-A_0$.
Thus the two boundary points are actual left-gap endpoints, and the other
noncentral point is the already forced supported endpoint. This constructs
$K_D\subset U_C$ for both gap ranks; the four-point lemma is its universal
candidate obstruction.

\paragraph{Scope of the retained calculations below.}
The local admissible cells, CE1 selected-return calculations, and the two
rescuer chart certificates below remain inputs to the fixed-witness proof.
The older one-Vd radial-separation estimates and the boundary-tail budget
are retained as alternative quantitative results, not as additional active
finite-enclosure cases. In particular, an actual-center separation is not
itself renamed as an unrestricted enclosure inequality.
