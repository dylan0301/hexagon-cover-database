\title[Seven triangles do not cover the unit hexagon]
{Seven Open Unit Equilateral Triangles Do Not Cover a Regular Unit Hexagon}
\author{Jineon Baek, Jeewon Kim}
\keywords{equilateral triangles, geometric covering, regular hexagon,
discharging, boundary propagation, area loss, exact computer-assisted certificate}
\subjclass[2020]{52C15, 52A40, 51M04}

\begin{document}
\raggedbottom

\begin{abstract}
We prove that the regular hexagon of side length one cannot be covered by
seven open equilateral triangles of side length one.  Seven distinguished
points force one center role and six vertex roles.  A finite classification
then routes every hypothetical cover to one of four geometric mechanisms:
trace deficit, diagonal discharging, area loss, or a nine-point support
obstruction.  In the discharging mechanism, diagonal demands release
state-dependent boundary charge governed by exact local \(g\)-maps; center
intervals absorb only their connected contribution, and ordinary
nonsupercritical V triangles transport the remaining charge to a terminal
capacity or threshold contradiction.  Support-line derivations, polynomial
reductions, monotonicity calculations, and exact certificates are isolated in
technical appendices.  The two final mixed cap overlaps are verified by a
formally incorporated exact symbolic certificate using integer, rational, and
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) arithmetic.
\end{abstract}
