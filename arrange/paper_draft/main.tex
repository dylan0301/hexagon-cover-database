\title[Seven triangles do not cover the unit hexagon]
{Seven Open Unit Equilateral Triangles Do Not Cover a Regular Unit Hexagon}
\author{Jineon Baek, Jeewon Kim}
\keywords{equilateral triangles, geometric covering, regular hexagon,
boundary propagation, area loss, exact computer-assisted certificate}
\subjclass[2020]{52C15, 52A40, 51M04}

\begin{document}
\raggedbottom

\begin{abstract}
We prove that a regular hexagon of side length one cannot be covered by seven
open equilateral triangles of side length one.  The center and the six vertices
canonically label the seven covering triangles.  We classify the possible
boundary traces of the center triangle and the possible radial incidences of
each vertex triangle.  Every resulting case is excluded by one of four
arguments: a trace-length bound, propagation of boundary-reach lower bounds,
an area-loss estimate, or an equilateral-enclosure obstruction for nine forced
points.  The final two algebraic inequalities are verified by an exact symbolic
certificate over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$.
\end{abstract}
