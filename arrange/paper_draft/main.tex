\begin{abstract}
We prove that the regular hexagon of side length one cannot be covered by
seven open equilateral triangles of side length one.  The center and six
vertices canonically label the seven covering triangles, and an exhaustive
classification routes every hypothetical cover to a trace-length, area-loss,
or finite equilateral-enclosure contradiction.  The body gives the exact
geometric and set-theoretic definitions and the compact interfaces used by
these three mechanisms.  Appendices~A--F solve the associated optimization
and feasibility problems.  The zero-gap nine-point terminal culminates in an
exact polynomial positivity certificate over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$; numerical
scans are not proof dependencies.
\end{abstract}
