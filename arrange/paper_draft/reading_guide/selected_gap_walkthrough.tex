\readerheading{Reading the six points.}
There are three sources of containment, which should be kept separate:
\begin{center}
\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{.43\linewidth}p{.49\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Points & Why the original C triangle contains them \\
\midrule
$M_0$ & Structural anchor: the distinguished C midpoint. \\
$X_0(B_0),\ X_0(1-A_1)$ & The endpoints of the actual gap $J_0$;
all open V triangles miss them. \\
$\widehat P_2,\widehat P_3,\widehat P_4$ & Total radial endpoints,
including all permitted neighboring traces. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
A singleton gap merely makes the two boundary points coincide. No distinctness
assumption is needed.

The center $O$ is not an additional seventh point. To see this explicitly,
put $t=B_0$ and $G=X_0(t)$. The positive-coefficient identity
\[
 G+\frac{1-t}{d_2}\widehat P_2+\frac1{d_4}\widehat P_4=0
\]
from \zcref{lem:fixed-origin-in-hull}, after division by the sum of its
coefficients, expresses $O$ as a convex combination of three listed points.
Thus any open candidate containing the six points also contains $O$.

The enclosure proof now varies that candidate, not the points. Its gap
anchors force a positive boundary trace, so the candidate has one of the
two nonzero center types. The four gap-free middle edges propagate demands
along the original nonsupercritical path. The original tail bound
$B_5\ge B_0/2$ supplies the last input even when the candidate does not cover
the other incident edge. The two candidate calculations then show that
these demands cannot coexist with the candidate's radial exits.
