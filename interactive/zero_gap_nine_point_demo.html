20-second overview

Seven roles leave nowhere else for the witnesses to go

The problem asks whether a regular side-1 hexagon H can be covered by seven open side-1 equilateral triangles. Its six vertices and center are pairwise at least one unit apart, so no open unit triangle can contain two of them: six triangles must serve the vertices and the seventh must serve the center.

Strategy 4 subcase. The six vertex roles are Vd0 (no positive radial trace), together cover the full boundary, and exactly one actual boundary row is supercritical. Under these hypotheses, the proof constructs nine points rejected by every vertex role, so the cover forces them all into the center role.

Exact scope and branch terminology

The reusable obstruction assumes that six open Vd0 vertex roles cover the full boundary and exactly one actual boundary row is supercritical. After rotating that row to index 4, strict handoffs give x₃<x₂<x₁<x₀<x₅<x₄. The theorem is independent of the center class: it closes the CE0 application and the zero-gap CE1/CE2 applications. Vd0 means that a vertex role has no positive-length trace on either adjacent radial arm.
